# CNC Milling Machine – Spindle and Drive Maintenance Manual

## 1. Scope

This manual covers routine maintenance and troubleshooting of the main spindle, spindle bearings, coolant system and axis drives of vertical CNC milling machines in the plant. Nominal operating values: spindle speed 2400 rpm for standard roughing programs, spindle housing temperature 50–60 °C, overall vibration velocity below 2.8 mm/s RMS, spindle torque around 35 N·m and coolant pressure 6 bar.

## 2. Safety

Lock out and tag out the machine before opening the spindle housing or guarding. Allow the spindle to cool below 40 °C before touching the housing. Wear eye protection when handling coolant under pressure.

## 3. Spindle bearing failure – symptoms

Spindle bearing wear is the most common cause of unplanned downtime on milling machines. Early symptoms are a gradual rise in overall vibration velocity over several hours or days, often first noticed at the drive-end bearing. As wear progresses, friction increases and the spindle housing temperature rises with the vibration. Surface finish on machined parts deteriorates and chatter marks appear. A sudden vibration spike without a temperature change is more often caused by tool imbalance or a loose tool holder than by bearing damage.

## 4. Spindle bearing failure – diagnosis

Measure vibration at the drive-end and non-drive-end bearing positions in horizontal, vertical and axial directions. Compare against the baseline recorded at commissioning. Take an envelope (demodulated) spectrum: bearing defect frequencies (BPFO, BPFI, BSF) with harmonics confirm rolling-element damage. Check spindle run-out with a dial indicator; run-out above 5 µm at the spindle nose indicates bearing wear or contamination. Inspect the lubrication: discoloured, contaminated or insufficient grease accelerates wear.

## 5. Spindle bearing replacement

If overall vibration exceeds 4.5 mm/s RMS and is rising, schedule bearing replacement at the next planned stop. Above 7.1 mm/s the machine must be stopped to avoid catastrophic spindle seizure. Replace bearings as a matched set, never individually. Use the bearing preload specified on the spindle data plate. After replacement run the spindle warm-up cycle (10 minutes at 25%, 50% and 75% of maximum speed) and record a new vibration baseline.

## 6. Lubrication

Re-grease spindle bearings every 2000 operating hours with the specified high-speed spindle grease. Over-greasing causes heat build-up; under-greasing causes metal-to-metal contact. Check the oil-air lubrication unit level weekly on machines so equipped.

## 7. Spindle overheating

If spindle temperature rises above 75 °C without a corresponding rise in vibration, check the spindle chiller first: verify chiller set point, coolant level in the chiller tank, clogged chiller condenser fins and pump operation. Clean the condenser fins with compressed air monthly. A failed chiller fan or thermostatic valve leads to a slow, steady temperature climb.

## 8. Coolant pressure loss

Low coolant pressure (below 4.5 bar) leads to poor chip evacuation and tool overheating. Check the coolant tank level, clean the suction strainer and filter, and inspect hoses and fittings for leaks. A worn coolant pump impeller gives gradually falling pressure.

## 9. Overload and high torque

High spindle torque with falling speed indicates the cutting load is too high: blunt tools, incorrect feeds and speeds, or chip packing. Also check for mechanical binding in the drive belt or gearbox and verify that the motor current is within nameplate rating. Repeated overload trips damage the spindle drive.

## 10. Housing cracks and surface defects

Fatigue cracks can form in the spindle bearing housing and tool holder flanges after a bearing seizure or a crash. Any crack indication found by visual or camera inspection must be confirmed with dye-penetrant testing. A cracked housing must be replaced; it cannot be welded. Investigate the root cause, typically bearing failure or a collision, before restarting.
