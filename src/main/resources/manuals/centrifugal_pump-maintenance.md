# Centrifugal Pump – Operation and Maintenance Manual

## 1. Scope

Applies to the end-suction centrifugal coolant and process water pumps. Nominal duty: 2950 rpm, discharge pressure 4.0 bar, bearing housing temperature 40–50 °C, overall vibration below 2.8 mm/s RMS, shaft torque about 50 N·m.

## 2. Daily checks

Check discharge pressure and motor current against the duty point. Listen for unusual noise such as gravel-like sound, which indicates cavitation. Check the mechanical seal for drips.

## 3. Cavitation

Cavitation occurs when suction pressure falls below the vapour pressure of the liquid. Symptoms: crackling noise, fluctuating discharge pressure, rising broadband vibration and pitting damage on the impeller vanes. Remedy: clean the suction strainer, check that the suction valve is fully open, and verify the tank level gives enough net positive suction head.

## 4. Loss of discharge pressure

A gradual fall in discharge pressure is usually caused by impeller wear, an increased wear-ring clearance or a leaking mechanical seal. A sudden drop points to an air leak on the suction side, a blocked strainer or a broken coupling. Perform a leak check on the suction pipe joints, seal and fittings. Measure wear-ring clearance at the next overhaul; replace rings if the clearance exceeds twice the original value.

## 5. Bearing wear and vibration

Rising vibration at the bearing housing with increasing temperature indicates bearing wear or lubrication failure. Check oil level and condition in the bearing housing; replace oil that is milky (water ingress) or dark. If vibration exceeds 4.5 mm/s RMS, plan bearing replacement. Above 7.1 mm/s stop the pump. Also check shaft alignment with the motor; misalignment shows high axial vibration at twice running speed.

## 6. Overheating

High bearing temperature without vibration change is commonly caused by over-lubrication, the wrong oil grade, or running the pump against a closed discharge valve. Never run the pump dry or against a closed valve for more than one minute.

## 7. Impeller inspection

During overhaul, inspect the impeller for erosion, cavitation pitting and cracks at the vane roots. Cracked impellers must be replaced. Pitting deeper than 2 mm requires replacement and investigation of the suction conditions.
