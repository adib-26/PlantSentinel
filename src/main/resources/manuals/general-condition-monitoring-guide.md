# Plant Condition Monitoring and Maintenance Guide

## 1. Vibration severity zones

Overall vibration velocity (mm/s RMS, 10–1000 Hz) is assessed in four zones, in line with the ISO 10816 approach for medium-sized machines on rigid foundations:

- Zone A, below 2.8 mm/s: newly commissioned machines, good condition.
- Zone B, 2.8 to 4.5 mm/s: acceptable for unrestricted long-term operation.
- Zone C, 4.5 to 7.1 mm/s: unsatisfactory. Operation may continue for a limited period until a suitable opportunity for remedial action. Plan maintenance.
- Zone D, above 7.1 mm/s: severe enough to cause damage. Stop the machine.

The trend matters as much as the absolute value. A doubling of vibration from the machine's own baseline warrants investigation even inside zone B.

## 2. Typical fault signatures

| Fault | Vibration | Temperature | Other signals |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bearing wear | Rising gradually | Rises later | Envelope spectrum defect frequencies |
| Imbalance | High at 1x running speed | Normal | Radial direction |
| Misalignment | High axial, 2x running speed | Coupling warm | |
| Cooling failure | Normal | Rising steadily | Fan/cooler issues |
| Overload or jam | May rise | Rises | High torque, speed drop |
| Leak | Normal | Normal | Pressure falling |

## 3. Preventive maintenance schedule

Routine inspections follow the preventive maintenance schedule: daily operator checks, weekly lubrication checks, monthly condition monitoring rounds, and annual overhauls. Continue routine monitoring when no fault signature is present.

## 4. Responding to anomalies

When the monitoring system raises an anomaly:

1. Confirm the reading is genuine (sensor fault, loose mounting, process change).
2. Compare with the machine's own baseline and the trend over the past hours.
3. Correlate with other channels to identify the fault signature.
4. Consult the machine-specific manual for diagnosis steps.
5. Raise a maintenance recommendation with priority based on severity and failure risk.
6. A qualified engineer approves the recommendation before work is scheduled.
7. After the work, record the findings and close the incident with a report.

## 5. Crack and surface defect inspections

Visual and camera-based inspections screen components for cracks, scratches and pitting. Automated detections are screening results only. Confirm every crack indication with a non-destructive test (dye-penetrant for non-magnetic parts, magnetic particle for ferritic steel) before replacing the component. Quarantine suspect parts so they cannot be refitted.
